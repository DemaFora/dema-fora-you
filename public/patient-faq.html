About the Program
What is ketamine therapy and how does it work?

Ketamine is an FDA-approved anesthetic that has been shown in clinical research to produce rapid antidepressant and anxiolytic effects when used at sub-anesthetic doses. It works through a different mechanism than traditional antidepressants — primarily through the glutamate system — which is why it can work when other treatments have not. Many patients notice improvement within hours to days of their first session.

Who is this treatment for?

Ketamine therapy may be appropriate for adults experiencing treatment-resistant depression, anxiety, PTSD, or chronic pain — particularly those who have not found adequate relief from other approaches. Your provider will conduct a thorough evaluation to determine if you are a good candidate.

How is treatment delivered?

All consultations and follow-ups are conducted via telehealth with licensed providers. Depending on your program, treatment may be administered in a supervised at-home setting or referred to a local infusion center. Your care coordinator will walk you through the specific protocol for your program.

How many sessions will I need?

Most patients begin with an initial series of sessions — typically six over two to three weeks. Maintenance sessions may follow, spaced further apart. The frequency and total number of sessions is determined by your provider based on your clinical response.

How effective is it?

Clinical research shows significant response rates in patients with treatment-resistant depression and PTSD. Results vary by individual — some patients experience dramatic improvement after a single session; others respond more gradually. No specific outcome is guaranteed. Your provider will monitor your progress and adjust your treatment plan accordingly.

What to Expect
What does a session feel like?

Most patients experience a mild to moderate altered state — a sense of floating, perceptual changes, or a dreamlike quality. Some find it deeply relaxing; others find it mildly disorienting. The experience typically lasts 45 to 60 minutes. You remain conscious and can communicate with your care team if needed. The altered state fully resolves within 1 to 2 hours after the session ends.

Will I have someone with me during sessions?

We strongly recommend having a trusted person present or available during your session, particularly for the first few sessions. Your care team will be reachable by phone. A trained integration support provider may also be available as an optional add-on.

How will I feel afterward?

Most patients feel tired, calm, or mildly foggy for a few hours after a session. Some feel a sense of emotional clarity or relief fairly quickly. Avoid driving, important decisions, and alcohol for at least 24 hours. Full instructions are in your Pre and Post-Treatment guide.

What if I have a difficult experience during a session?

Challenging experiences — emotional or perceptual — can occur. They are generally brief and resolve on their own. Your care team will prepare you in advance with guidance on how to navigate them. Having a safe, comfortable environment and a trusted person nearby significantly reduces distress. If you are concerned during a session, you can communicate with your support person.

Logistics and Practical Questions
Can I continue my current medications?

In most cases, yes. Your provider will review your medication list and advise if any adjustments are needed. Do not stop or change any prescription medication without guidance from your prescribing physician.

Does insurance cover this?

At this time we do not bill insurance directly. Some patients are eligible for out-of-network reimbursement — we can provide a detailed receipt (superbill) upon request. HSA and FSA funds are generally eligible. Coverage varies by plan; we recommend contacting your insurer directly to ask about mental health benefits for out-of-network providers.

How do I reschedule or cancel an appointment?

Contact your care coordinator as early as possible. Cancellations made more than 48 hours in advance are not charged. Late cancellations may be subject to a fee. See your Financial Agreement for full details.

Can I stop treatment if I want to?

Yes, at any time. Please inform your care coordinator so your provider can ensure a safe transition. There is no obligation to continue and your privacy and records are protected regardless of your decision.

How do I get my medical records?

Contact your care coordinator to request a copy of your records. We aim to fulfill record requests within 10 business days. A small administrative fee may apply.

If you are in crisis, do not wait for your next appointment. Call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or call 911 if you are in immediate danger. DemaFora You is not an emergency service.
